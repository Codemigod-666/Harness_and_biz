# Discovery conversations: script

Goal: learn how they work today and what it costs them. **Don't pitch.** 30 minutes, in person if possible.
Talk about their past and present, not "would you use…". Opinions about the future are cheap; what they
already do and pay for is real.

## Opening (2 min)

"I'm building simple software for <distributors / schools> and want to understand how you run things today
before I build anything. No sales, I'm just trying to learn. Can I ask a few questions?"

## Core questions (both verticals)

1. Walk me through a normal day. Where does most of your (or your staff's) time go?
2. What's the most annoying or time-consuming part of that?
3. When did that last cause a real problem? What happened, and what did it cost?
4. How do you handle it today? (Tally, Excel, register, WhatsApp, a person?)
5. Have you tried anything else to fix it? Why did you stop, or why not?
6. What software do you pay for today, and roughly how much?
7. Who else would be involved in a decision like this?

## Distributor: dig into

- How do retailers place orders today? (Salesman visit, phone call, WhatsApp text, voice note?)
- How do orders get into Tally/billing? Who types them, and how long does it take?
- How do you track outstanding payments and chase collections? How many days of credit actually go unpaid?
- How do you know stock levels? Have you lost a sale to a stock-out, or carried dead stock?
- Does your accountant or CA insist on Tally?

## School: dig into

- How do you collect fees? What share is late, and how do you follow up?
- How do you send notices to parents? In which language?
- How is attendance taken and shared?
- What software do you use for admissions, fees or exams? What do you pay?
- When do you decide on tools for the next academic year, and who decides?

## Closing (3 min)

- "If I built something that fixed <their top pain>, would you try it with me for a month?"
- "Would you pay a small amount for that pilot?" Listen for a number.
- "Who else like you should I talk to?" (Ask for an introduction, not just a name.)

## Signals

| Strong yes | Polite no |
|---|---|
| Describes a recent, costly incident | "Interesting", "nice idea" |
| Already pays for, or has hacked together, a fix | Never tried to solve it |
| Agrees to a paid pilot or names a price | "Let me know when it's ready" |
| Introduces you to a peer on the spot | No introduction |

## Note sheet (one per conversation)

```
Date / who / role / business size:
Top 3 pains (their words):
Last real incident + cost:
Current tools + monthly spend:
Decision maker:
Pilot yes/no + price mentioned:
Introductions given:
My read (strong / weak / no):
```

Score after 8: the vertical with the most "strong" sheets and a pilot yes wins. If there are none, change the
question, not the product.
